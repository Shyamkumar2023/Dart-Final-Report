Feeder Electronics

Design requirements

Indexing accuracy

The feed motor must stop at well-defined angular positions so the physical magazine aligns with the launcher loading bay after each cycle.

Transfer torque

Servos must hold and lower the dart without stalling; the design targets on the order of 2 N·m continuous capability at the chosen reduction and horn geometry.

Handoff synchronisation

The feeder may only advance the dart when the launcher carriage is in a known pose—signalled when drawing the band trips a datum sensor—so hooks and dart features do not collide.

Non-contact datum

The loading cue should avoid mechanical limit switches at the interface where possible, to reduce wear, bounce, and maintenance.

Tunability

Distances, servo angles, and feeder motor angles must be adjustable without reflashing full mission code, because mechanical stack-up varies across builds.

State coordination

The first shot differs from follow-on shots (initial ready gating); firmware must encode that distinction explicitly.

Components and selection

The magazine uses a Damiao integrated indexer and TIANKONGRC digital servos for dart transfer; each major electromechanical part includes a photo reference and a transcribed datasheet summary. Remaining interface hardware is summarized in the cards at the end of this section.

Feeder indexer — Damiao DM4340

Integrated brushless servo actuator (motor + driver + dual encoders)

Drives the feeder magazine to the next cell and holds intermediate positions during the load sequence. Control uses onboard CAN at 1 Mbps; factory tuning uses UART at 921600 baud. Dual 14-bit magnetic encoders on the module provide shaft-referenced feedback for indexing without a separate encoder assembly.

Dart transfer servos — TIANKONGRC TD-8120MG

Digital high-torque servo · 20 kg·cm class · PWM control

These servos clamp and release the dart during the feeder–launcher handoff. Stall torque reaches 26.8 kg·cm at 7.2 V (≈2.6 N·m at the horn), exceeding the ≈2 N·m design target with margin. Drive is standard RC PWM (500–2500 µs); firmware maps tuning angles to pulse width during bring-up. Retail labeling may quote 180° travel; the manufacturer datasheet below lists 360° continuous operation within the PWM band—use the configuration that matches your horn travel and mechanical stops.

Launcher datum — mechanical limit switch vs optical endstop

Option study for detecting “feeder may move” carriage pose

The feeder must know when the launcher carriage has reached a safe pose before indexing and dart transfer. Two common classes of sensors were compared: a subminiature roller-lever microswitch (contact) and a slot-type optical interrupter on a breakout board (non-contact).

Why the optical endstop was chosen

For the feeder–launcher handshake, the design prioritizes a non-contact datum (see design requirements): the carriage should not have to push a lever every cycle while the band system is under tension and vibration. The optical interrupter removes mechanical wear and contact bounce from the critical “feeder enabled” edge, improving repeatability of when the feeder motor and servos are allowed to move. That simplifies the state machine and reduces the risk of false triggers during tuning. The trade-off—keeping the optical slot clean and shielded from ambient light—is accepted and handled with mechanical shrouding and inspection during maintenance. The mechanical switch remains a valid backup concept for harsh dust environments but was not selected for this interface.

Optical endstop (build)

Slot sensor on a small breakout (photos and comparison above) generates the feeder-clear signal when the launcher carriage occludes the beam. Routed to the feeder PCB with short returns next to logic ground.

Feeder PCB

Aggregates CAN to the DM4340, servo drive signals, endstop conditioning, and returns to the wire-management layer for a single serviceable harness exit.

Implementation

The PCB routes power and signals from the robot backbone to the feeder motor driver, servos, and optical sensor. Ground and return paths are kept short to limit noise on the endstop channel during launcher vibration. Firmware implements a guarded state machine: after the first launch, a ready handshake allows the magazine to index; the BLDC rotates until the next cell is aligned; once the launcher has advanced and the optical beam breaks, servos lower the dart; the launcher captures hooks, backs off to a hook-clear pose, and the feeder rotates clear of the launch line.

A compile-time directive selects autonomous operation versus tuning mode. In tuning, motion to each of three taught index positions can be exercised in isolation, and live expressions update distances and angles until values are frozen for competition firmware.

Testing

Indexing and encoder

Bench tests step the feeder motor between the three index commands and compare commanded steps to onboard 14-bit encoder deltas; mismatches indicate mechanical slip or calibration drift before integration with the launcher.

Optical endstop vs launcher pose

With the launcher driven slowly forward, the endstop transition is logged against carriage position or motor counts to confirm the feeder is only enabled inside the intended window.

Servo transfer

Static load trials with a mass representative of the dart verify that servos hold position without hunting and release without rebound into the dart path.

End-to-end cycle

Full sequences are run from first ready through multi-shot loading to confirm the state machine matches the mechanical choreography (hook engagement, clearance rotation, non-interference on fire).

Tuning mode regression

After parameter changes in tuning mode, a short autonomous regression is executed to ensure stored limits still satisfy the same endstop and hook-clear predicates.
