Iterative Optimization

The Path to Darkstar

Achieving ballistic stability at 25m/s is a task of systematic refinement. Before the V7 Darkstar reached its definitive form, its predecessors underwent an exhaustive computational gauntlet. Through six distinct generations, we mapped the fluid dynamics of every fin geometry, fuselage profile and main body structure.

By leveraging high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), we identified and eliminated critical aerodynamic pitfalls—from early-stage structural flutter in the V1 Thorn to boundary layer separation issues in the V6 Trident. This data-driven evolution was essential to ensure the direct-drive servos could maintain control authority during the high-G terminal phase.

The V7 Darkstar marks a shift from structural prototyping to a fully integrated flight platform. Engineering a 260g All-Up Weight (AUW) required balancing the protection of internal PCBs with high-speed accessibility for field operations.

Survival Statistics

After 167 consecutive high-velocity launches, the V7 Darkstar fuselage showed zero visible stress fractures. In comparison, the TPU 95A control models exhibited critical tearing at the head-to-fuselage interface within 20 launches.

Empirical Control Validation

Servo Fin Sensitivity Analysis

The Carbon Paper Methodology

To validate control surface sensitivity before autonomous tracking, we pre-programmed yaw deflections of 0°, 4°, 8°, 12°, 16°, and 20°. By launching into carbon paper layered over plain sheets, we captured the exact impact coordinates via ink displacement.

Analysis: 0° deflection demonstrated extreme precision. As fin angle increased, we observed a linear deviation to the right, confirming that the projectile's mechanical sensitivity scale is highly predictable—even as turbulence reduces grouping at high angles.
