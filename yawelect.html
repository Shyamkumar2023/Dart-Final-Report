Methodology used

The development process followed a comprehensive V-shaped system methodology. This approach enabled the structured design, implementation, and rigorous unit testing of each distinct hardware and software (yaw, pitch, launcher, feeder) subsystem in isolation. Once isolated tests were verified, these elements were incrementally integrated and validated on the opposite side of the "V", culminating in a robust, fully functional final system.

Yaw Electronics Design Requirements

Yaw travel envelope

Approximately 7.8° to one side and 2.2° to the other from centre, matching the mechanical aim window without exceeding bearing or cable-wrap limits.

Operator safety

Software limits prevent the turret from entering the lateral space where teammates or judges can stand; commands that would violate the envelope are rejected or clamped.

Closed-loop position

Encoder feedback is required so yaw holding torque and drift are compensated during long match segments.

Operator display — capacitive vs resistive touch

4.0″ class modules · SPI LCD · touch overlay technology trade-off

The pit crew UI needs a readable panel that works with gloved or dry fingers under time pressure. Two representative 4.0″ module styles were compared: a capacitive SPI TFT with I²C touch (ST7796S + FT6336U class, as documented on LCD Wiki — 4.0″ capacitive SPI ST7796) and a conventional resistive 4.0″ assembly that relies on pressure and often ships with a stylus.

Why capacitive was chosen

For field adjustments next to a running launcher, the team prioritized fast, low-effort taps without hunting for a stylus, plus a brighter, cleaner readout for numbers under venue lighting. The 4.0″ 320×480 capacitive SPI module family on LCD Wiki documents a compact 14-pin header (LCD SPI, backlight, SD_CS, and CTP I²C), onboard level shifting for 5 V / 3.3 V MCUs, and an SD slot for assets—matching the RoboMaster controller wiring budget better than a resistive panel that pushes the operator toward stylus-based workflows. Resistive remains a valid low-cost fallback where gloves are thick or where EMI noise on the analog sense lines is easier to manage than capacitive moisture edge cases.

Yaw Electronics Implementation

The yaw subsystem is built around the Damiao DM-J10010L-2EC integrated servo actuator, which provides the required torque and precision within the compact turret envelope. Its built-in dual absolute magnetic encoders support closed-loop position control without additional sensing hardware, which simplifies the design and improves reliability. This allows the subsystem to achieve the required angular control range of approximately 7.8° to one side and 2.2° to the other from centre. Control commands are sent over a dedicated CAN bus at 1 Mbps, while parameter tuning is performed via a high-speed UART interface at 921600 bps. For operator safety, software clamps are implemented to restrict motion within the defined allowable range.

All Subsystems Electronics Requirements

Once each subsystem has been verified in isolation, the integration layer must satisfy additional system-level requirements for the overall system to fulfil the project objectives

CAN bus arbitration

All actuators (yaw DM-J10010L, pitch motor driver, feeder DM4340) share a single 1 Mbps CAN segment. IDs must be assigned without collision, and worst-case bus loading must leave headroom for time-critical position frames during a launch cycle.

Power rail integrity

The 24 V distribution bus must supply peak stall currents of all simultaneous consumers without the rail sagging below the minimum specified for any actuator driver. Decoupling at each PCB must keep ripple within datasheet limits.

EMC / cross-subsystem noise

Launcher flywheel switching transients must not corrupt encoder edges on the pitch axis or endstop signals on the feeder during simultaneous operation. Ground paths on each PCB must be routed to minimise common-impedance coupling.

Safe sequencing across subsystems

The launch command may only execute when yaw, pitch, and feeder all report a ready state. A firmware interlock on the RoboMaster controller must gate the launcher trigger on all three acknowledgements to prevent partial-state firing.

Harness serviceability

All inter-board connectors must be keyed or colour-coded so no harness can be mis-inserted within a match cycle. The wire management PCB must survive at least one full competition day of repeated insertion cycles without derating.

Thermal management

Motor drivers and power distribution components must not exceed their thermal ratings during back-to-back match sequences. Placement and airflow must be evaluated to confirm steady-state junction temperatures remain within safe margins.

Robot-Level Electronics Implementation

All four subsystem PCBs plug into the wire management layer via keyed connectors, creating a single serviceable harness exit from the turret. The 24 V rail is distributed from the RoboMaster power supply through a common fused branch point; each PCB carries its own bulk capacitance and ferrite bead filtering to decouple switching transients between the launcher flywheel drive and the position-sensing circuits.

The shared CAN bus at 1 Mbps connects the RoboMaster Development Board A to the yaw actuator, feeder actuator, and the launcher flywheel driver. Node IDs are assigned statically so the host can detect a missing acknowledgement within one control cycle and escalate a fault before commanding motion. Pitch uses a separate PWM and encoder path rather than CAN, keeping the CAN utilisation budget below 50% even at full refresh rate.

A software interlock layer in the RoboMaster firmware collects the ready flags from all subsystems before gating the launch trigger. Flags are only set when positional targets are reached within tolerance and the feeder handshake has completed; any subsystem fault clears the launch permission and illuminates the operator display.

Ground planes on each PCB are stitched via vias at the power connector so differential return currents stay local to each board. Signal cables between boards are routed away from the flywheel motor leads; where physical separation is limited, twisted-pair or shielded lines are used for endstop and encoder signals.

Robot-Level Electronics Testing

CAN bus load and arbitration

A logic analyser or CAN sniffer captures all frames during a simulated match sequence. Bus utilisation is calculated; no frame should be delayed by more than one additional bit-time due to arbitration loss, and no ID collision or error frame should appear during normal operation.

Rail sag under combined load

All actuators are commanded simultaneously to peak demand while the 24 V rail is monitored on an oscilloscope. Voltage must not sag below 22 V at any PCB connector; ripple amplitude must remain within the maximum specified in each driver datasheet.

Cross-subsystem EMC

The launcher flywheels are spun to operating speed while the pitch encoder and feeder optical endstop are continuously monitored. Any spurious encoder edge or endstop false-trigger during launcher operation constitutes a failure; the threshold is zero unintended transitions in a 60 s window.

Interlock gate verification

With each subsystem individually held in a non-ready state (yaw outside its sector, feeder mid-index, pitch not yet homed), a launch command is issued; the interlock must suppress the trigger in every case. When all subsystems simultaneously report ready, the launch must proceed within the defined response latency.

Multi-shot endurance cycle

A sequence of repeated aim-and-fire cycles is run to simulate competition usage. All subsystem PCBs are monitored for thermal rise; temperatures are logged to confirm that board surface temperatures do not approach component derating points after the longest anticipated match block.

Connector and harness integrity

All harness connectors are disconnected and reconnected the minimum number of times expected over the competition day and re-examined for fretting, deformed contacts, or increased mating resistance. Continuity and insulation resistance are re-checked after cycling to confirm the wire management layer remains within specification.
